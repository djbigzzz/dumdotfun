The idea is interesting, but as an application this is not grant-ready yet. Right now it reads more like a narrative about memecoin psychology than a clearly specified protocol with defined components, risks, and deliverables.

Here are the main gaps you need to address:

• It’s not clear what the product actually is.
Is MissOut a smart contract protocol, a consumer app, an SDK, or a platform?
Is it permissionless infrastructure or an application users interact with directly?

• The concrete components are missing.
What contracts exist?
What does the front end do, if any?
What is on-chain vs off-chain?
What are the actual primitives being built?

• Who interacts with it and how.
Who creates pools?
Who joins them?
Who settles them?
What happens step-by-step from a user’s point of view?

• There’s no clear “end state” for the grant.
What exists at the end of funding?
Is it a deployed protocol on mainnet?
A working MVP with real users?
Audited contracts?

• There is no explicit “why grant funding” case.
At the moment it reads as “we’re building a company and want help funding an MVP.”
You need to explain why this should be funded as ecosystem infrastructure, not just a startup.

• No risk section.
There’s no discussion of technical risks (randomness, manipulation, griefing, oracle assumptions),
no economic risks,
and no compliance or consumer-risk considerations given this is effectively a wagering mechanic.

• No budget breakdown.
Grants require clarity on what money is actually being spent on.
Engineering vs audits vs infra vs UX.

• The milestones are framed narratively, not technically.
They should be framed around protocol progress, similar to Coldstar:
core contracts → security assumptions → usability → adoption → standardisation.

• The “why this should exist” argument needs work.
You need to answer:
Why should this exist even if MissOut as a company disappears?
What does the Solana ecosystem gain long-term from this primitive?

Overall: the core mechanic might be valid, but the application needs to move from concept to specification. Once it’s concrete enough that a reviewer can clearly picture the protocol, the risks, and the outcome of funding, it becomes much easier to justify a grant.
